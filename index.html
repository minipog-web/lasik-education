HOW EYES FOCUS Focal Plane Biophysics

Why Nearsighted & Farsighted Eyes Blur

Your cornea handles two-thirds of your eye's optical power. In nearsightedness (myopia), an overly steep cornea focuses light rays in front of the retina. In farsightedness (hyperopia), rays overshoot behind it. Our CustomVue® laser reshapes this front curve by microscopic microns—shifting the focal plane directly onto your retina for effortless 20/20 clarity.

CORNEA SHAPE Toric & Iris Tracking

Astigmatism: The "Football vs. Basketball" Curve

A normal cornea is spherical like a basketball, bending light equally in all directions. In astigmatism, the cornea is toric like a football—curved more steeply along one axis than the other. This splits incoming light into two separate focal points, creating ghosted text, shadows, and night halos. 3D Iris Tracking balances both curves into a single, crisp focal plane.

However, around age 40–45, the eye's natural lens gradually loses flexibility (a universal condition known as presbyopia), meaning reading glasses will eventually be needed for close-up tasks. Monovision LASIK (correcting one eye for distance and the other for near vision) is only considered in cases where patients have already demonstrated an established ability to function with different focal distances (such as through prior successful monovision contact lens wear) or are naturally hyperopic in one eye and myopic in the other. Monovision is not well tolerated without this prior history, which is why Dr. Marano and Dr. Raouf recommend it exclusively in specific, carefully screened candidates.
